Chapter 6 · Reliability

Reliability: will the data last until the last day?

Testing only proves a chip is good on the day it ships. Reliability asks whether, over five or ten years in a user’s hands — or fifteen in a car — charge will leak away, neighbouring rows will disturb each other, cosmic rays will flip bits, or oxides will wear out.

The bathtub curve and FIT

  • FIT (failures in time): failures per 10⁹ device-hours. 1 FIT ≈ one failure every 114,000 years for a single device.
  • MTTF = 10⁹ / FIT hours. In a system of 1000 devices, FITs add up.
  • Memory also separates hard errors (permanent damage) from soft errors (a bit flips but the device is fine; rewriting fixes it).

DRAM retention

Cell retention times have a long tail: almost all cells hold data for seconds, but a few in each chip last only tens of milliseconds. The refresh period exists for those tail cells.

  • Temperature: leakage (especially junction leakage) follows an Arrhenius law, and retention roughly halves for each 10 °C rise. That is why DRAM above 85 °C must refresh twice as often.
  • VRT (variable retention time): some cells randomly jump between two or three retention times, thought to come from defects near the drain changing state. It makes “test once to find the weak cells” unreliable and is one reason retention-aware refresh schemes are hard to deploy.
  • Data-pattern dependence: neighbouring data changes leakage paths, so retention tests use several data backgrounds.

Row hammer

Research in 2014 confirmed that repeatedly activating one row makes cells in neighbouring rows lose data before refresh. Mechanisms include capacitive coupling from the word line toggling and electrons injected and migrating into neighbouring storage nodes. It is not just a reliability problem but a security hole: an attacker’s user program can flip page-table bits in the kernel and gain privileges.

  • Worse with scaling: the activation count needed to flip a bit (hammer count threshold) has fallen from over 100k in the DDR3 era to a few thousand in newer generations.
  • Variants: Half-Double (the row two away is also affected), RowPress (not many activations but keeping the aggressor open for long).
  • Mitigations:
    • TRR (target row refresh): the DRAM tracks suspicious rows and refreshes their neighbours, but tracking capacity is limited and has been shown to be bypassable.
    • RFM (refresh management, DDR5 / LPDDR5): the controller issues extra refresh commands based on each bank’s activation count.
    • PRAC (per-row activation counting, new in DDR5): every row has a counter; past a threshold the DRAM asks the controller (via ALERT) for time to mitigate. The cost is longer timings and area.
    • ECC reduces but does not eliminate the risk; systems also isolate memory and limit activation rates.

Flash endurance

Every program and erase pushes electrons through the tunnel oxide under a high field. Over time traps form in the oxide:

  • Trapped charge shifts and widens Vt distributions, and program / erase slow down (more ISPP pulses).
  • Defects form leakage paths (SILC, stress-induced leakage current), degrading retention.
  • Eventually the Vt window closes and states can no longer be told apart.

So endurance and retention go together: the more a block has been cycled, the worse it retains data. SSD TBW ratings are defined as “after the rated number of cycles, still retain data for a given time at a given temperature”.

Flash data retention

  • Mechanisms: charge leaks through tunnel-oxide defects (Vt drops), or charge in shallow traps escapes (detrapping). In 3D NAND the trap layer is continuous along the channel, adding lateral charge migration into the space between cells. A fast Vt shift shortly after programming (early retention loss) must also be handled.
  • Temperature acceleration: hotter storage loses charge faster; programming temperature matters too (cross-temperature: program cold and read hot, or vice versa, shifts Vt).
  • JEDEC (JESD218): a client SSD written to its rated TBW must retain data for 1 year powered off at 30 °C; enterprise drives for 3 months at 40 °C.
  • Countermeasures: background scans (patrol read) find blocks whose error count approaches the ECC limit and rewrite them elsewhere (refresh / relocation); read voltages are adjusted dynamically (read retry).

Read and program disturb

  • Read disturb: during a read, unselected cells on the string see VPASS (about 6–8 V); repeated reads slightly program them and shift Vt up, especially in the erased state. Read-hot blocks must be relocated periodically.
  • Program disturb: inhibited cells on the same word line, and other cells on the string, see VPGM or VPASS and can be programmed by accident.
  • Cell-to-cell interference: charge written into neighbours shifts a cell’s Vt through parasitic capacitance. Controllers compensate with program order (e.g. coarse then fine multi-pass programming).
  • SRAM read disturb: see Devices; a circuit-level stability issue.

Soft errors

An energetic particle entering silicon creates electron–hole pairs; if the collected charge exceeds a node’s critical charge Qcrit, the bit flips.

  • Sources: alpha particles from trace radioactive elements in package materials and solder; atmospheric neutrons from cosmic rays (more at altitude, in aircraft and in space); thermal neutrons reacting with boron-10.
  • SRAM is most sensitive: small node capacitance, low Qcrit. Scaling lowers the chance any one bit is hit (smaller area), but raises the share of strikes that flip several neighbouring bits (MBU / MCU).
  • DRAM: relatively large cell capacitance gives a lower per-bit soft-error rate, but the bit count is enormous; periphery and sense amps can be hit too.
  • Countermeasures: ECC with bit interleaving, low-alpha materials, hardened designs (e.g. DICE dual-interlocked cells), and memory scrubbing (periodically read, correct and write back so errors do not accumulate into multi-bit errors).
  • Soft-error rate is expressed in FIT/Mb and measured with accelerated neutron beams or alpha sources (JESD89).

Transistor and interconnect aging

  • BTI (bias temperature instability): long bias shifts Vt. SRAM cells holding the same value for a long time age asymmetrically, lowering SNM and raising Vmin.
  • HCI (hot carrier injection): carriers in high fields damage the oxide interface, affecting high-speed periphery and I/O.
  • TDDB (time-dependent dielectric breakdown): gate oxides or capacitor dielectrics break down after long field stress; DRAM capacitor dielectrics and VPP high-voltage circuits are the focus.
  • Electromigration (EM): high current density moves metal atoms, causing opens or shorts in power grids and TSVs / bumps.

Acceleration models and FIT math

Reliability tests simulate years of use in weeks through an acceleration factor (AF). Temperature acceleration uses the Arrhenius model:

AF = exp[ (Ea/k) · (1/Tuse − 1/Tstress) ]　　k = 8.617×10⁻⁵ eV/K

Example: Ea = 0.7 eV, use at 55 °C (328 K), stress at 125 °C (398 K) → AF ≈ e4.35 ≈ 78. 1000 hours of HTOL ≈ 78,000 hours ≈ 8.9 years of use.

With zero failures, the failure-rate upper bound comes from the chi-square distribution:

λ ≤ χ²(CL, 2f+2) / (2 · N · t · AF)

231 units, 1000 hours, AF 78, 0 failures, 60% confidence (χ² = 1.83) → λ ≤ 1.83 / (2 × 231 × 1000 × 78) ≈ 5.1×10⁻⁸ /h ≈ 51 FIT. Voltage acceleration uses exponential or power-law models; humidity acceleration commonly uses the Peck model.

Glossary

FIT
Failures per 10⁹ device-hours.
MTTF
Mean time to failure.
VRT
Variable retention time: DRAM cells whose retention jumps randomly.
Row hammer
Repeatedly activating a row flips bits in neighbouring rows.
RowPress
Keeping a row open for long flips bits in neighbouring rows.
TRR / RFM / PRAC
Three DRAM row-hammer mitigation mechanisms.
P/E cycle
One program plus one erase.
SILC
Stress-induced leakage current.
Detrapping
Escape of trapped charge.
Lateral migration
Charge spreading sideways along the trap layer in 3D NAND.
Read disturb
Slight programming of unselected cells by repeated reads.
RBER
Raw bit error rate before ECC.
Qcrit
The minimum collected charge that flips a node.
MBU / MCU
Multi-bit / multi-cell upsets from one particle event.
Scrubbing
Periodically reading, correcting and writing back to stop errors accumulating.
BTI / HCI / TDDB / EM
The four main device and interconnect aging mechanisms.
HTOL
High-temperature operating life test.
Arrhenius
Exponential dependence of reaction rate on temperature, used for thermal acceleration.
Ea
Activation energy, which sets how strong thermal acceleration is.
